What this Extension is

Luma Pay Shopping Agent is a browser extension that adds a panel beside the page you are shopping on. The panel reads the codes, offers and prices that the store itself publishes, and keeps a local record of what it found. It does not place an order, does not complete a checkout and does not hold a card number. Its account and its balances live in this browser profile, not on a server.

The panel works only on the shops in the catalog it ships with, and on shops you add yourself with Check & add this site. On any other page it does nothing at all, and it reads nothing from that page. The Terms of Use that you agree to on the first screen are the agreement; this page is the practical guide to it.

Nothing in the Extension runs before you tick the box on the notice screen and choose Agree and continue. That includes the ad blocking, which stays off for a profile that has not agreed even though the switch reads on.

Opening the panel

Click the Luma Pay button on the browser toolbar, or press Alt+Shift+S. In Opera the panel opens in the sidebar; in other Chromium browsers it opens in the side panel. The panel belongs to the tab you are in, so opening a shop gives the panel something to work on.

If the panel opens on its notice screen, the Terms have not been accepted in this browser profile, or a newer version of them has been published since you last agreed. Read the notice, tick the empty box and choose Agree and continue. If the panel opens on the sign-in card, the profile has agreed to the Terms but has no account on this device yet.

If the button is not on the toolbar, open your browser's extensions page, find Luma Pay Shopping Agent, and pin it. If the shortcut collides with another extension's shortcut, change one of them on the browser's own shortcuts page; the panel opens either way.

First run

The first screen is the notice: what the panel asks your browser for, how it is paid for, and what stays on this device. Nothing else runs until you tick the empty box and choose Agree and continue. After agreeing, create an account on this device — an email address, a password and your date of birth. There is no Luma Pay server, so the account lives only in this browser profile.

A short tour then opens on that panel: seven steps covering codes, cashback, prices, the optional face scan, ads, and the shortcut for your computer. Use Next and Back, or Skip tour; it picks up where it stopped if you close the panel, and Replay the tour in Settings opens it again.

Your password is kept only as a salted hash, so it cannot be read back — not by you, not by anyone who opens the profile. There is also no reset by email, because there is no server to send a reset from. If you lose the password, the only way back to the account is a sealed backup whose recovery key you kept, made from the Backup and recovery section of Settings before you lost access.

The date of birth you enter is the record of your age for the payout gate. Entering it does not verify it against a document; the optional face-based check described further down is the only other way the panel forms a view about age, and it is not required to use the codes, the cashback cards or the price history.

If you agreed to the Terms and then closed the panel, nothing is lost: the record of your agreement and your account are in the profile, and the panel opens on the shopping view the next time you open a covered shop.

Shopping with it

  • Coupons. Open a shop in the catalog and the panel lists the codes the store itself publishes. Choose one and the panel types it into the store's own promo field, then reports what the store's page said. It never checks out or pays for anything.
  • Ways to save. Under the codes, the panel lists storewide mechanics the store itself runs — a loyalty scheme, an outlet rail, a price-match policy — with an Open button to the store. These are not codes, and the list is re-checked hourly.
  • Staying fresh. Settings has a Keeping up to date section: the freshness line, an automatic-refresh switch, Update now and Replay the tour. With refresh on, the list is re-read hourly and a code with an end date drops out by itself.
  • Cashback. Rates published by cashback programmes appear as cards. Activate opens the tracked link that hands your visit to the programme; the reward is confirmed by the programme, not by the panel.
  • Price history. Prices on product pages you visit are recorded on this device, so the panel can show how a price has moved. A 90-day low needs a price API you configure in Settings.
  • Ad value. With ad blocking on, requests to advertising and tracker domains are stopped in the shops the Extension covers, the empty slots are hidden, and an estimate of what that space was worth is credited to your wallet. Switch it off in Settings and all of that stops.
  • Wallet and payouts. Balances are a record of credits awarded. A payout needs a payout provider you configure in Settings and, for cash-out, a completed 18+ check.

The panel works one shop at a time. Open a product page in a covered shop and the panel picks the shop up from the address; open a page it does not cover and it says so instead of guessing. Codes belong to the store they were read from, so a code from one shop is never offered in another.

Nothing the panel does is a purchase step. Filling a code into a store's promo field changes the store's own basket, and the total in the store's cart is the amount that binds you — check it before you pay, because a code that the page accepted may still be limited, bundled or withdrawn later in the checkout.

Keeping the lists fresh

Two lists can refresh themselves. The catalog of covered shops and the published savings list are re-read hourly by the Extension's background worker while the browser is open, and a code with an end date drops out by itself when that date passes. The panel shows a freshness line so you can see when a list was last read.

Update now in Settings forces a refresh. Keeping up to date in Settings has the automatic-refresh switch; turning it off leaves the list as it is until you ask for an update or reopen the browser. A refresh is a request to the address the list was published at — or nothing at all if the list came with the package.

If the freshness line says the list is old, or a store you use is missing, force an update first, then reload the shop's page. A shop added with Check & add this site applies to the tab after a reload, not to the page you were on.

When something does nothing

  • The panel says the shop is not covered. The Extension runs on the shops in its catalog. Use Check & add this site and your browser asks for that one site; nothing else is granted.
  • No codes appear. Not every store publishes codes. Check the feature switch in Settings, and reload the page after adding the shop.
  • Ad value stays at zero. Ad blocking may be off, the page may carry no ad slots, or the per-page, per-tab or per-day ceiling may already be reached.
  • The age check will not pass. It needs a well-lit, straight-on view and a little movement through the angles it asks for. It is optional: with it incomplete, codes, cashback and prices still work and only cash-out stays locked.
  • Nothing at all runs. The panel does nothing until the terms are agreed. Read them from the notice screen and tick the box.
  • A feature that used to work stopped. A store can change its pages at any time, and a code, a price or a card can move out of reach until the Extension is updated for that page. Reload the page, force an update, and if it still fails, write to us with the store and what the panel shows.
  • The panel shows an error from a provider. An offer feed, a price history API or a payout provider answered with a refusal or did not answer at all. Check the address and credential in Settings; the panel reports what the provider said and does not retry against an address that failed.
  • A payout will not complete. It needs a configured payout provider address, a balance above that option's minimum, and — for cash-out — a completed 18+ check. A provider may also decline for its own reasons, in its own words, which the panel repeats.

Try the same steps in order before writing: reload the shop's page, open the panel again on that tab, force an update in Settings, and check that the feature's switch is on. Restarting the browser applies an update that is waiting. If none of that changes what you see, the message is worth sending — and what you include decides whether it can be answered.

Your data, and removing it

Everything the Extension keeps is in this browser profile. Export a copy writes it to a file, Clear saved codes and history deletes the shopping record, and removing the Extension deletes the rest with it. The Privacy Policy explains each item; the Terms of Use are the agreement itself.

Backups, and moving to another device

A browser profile can be wiped and a device can be lost, and everything the Extension knows goes with it. Backup and recovery in Settings seals the profile — account, wallet, saved and accepted codes, price points and settings — into one encrypted code, and locks it with a recovery key you are shown once.

Save an encrypted copy writes that code to a file you choose. If you also enter a sync address, the Extension posts the sealed code there after an earning and once an hour; the host receives ciphertext it cannot read. Provider credentials are left out of every copy, so a restored device asks for those again.

Keep the recovery key away from the copy itself, because the two together are the profile. Nobody can open a sealed copy without that key, including us: there is no escrow, no server copy and no reset. Restoring replaces what the profile holds and signs the device out.

The age check and cash-out

The 18+ check is optional and asks you separately before it starts. With no relay configured, everything happens on this device: you turn your head to a few angles, the panel measures small copies of the camera picture, and drops them. Only the verdict and the time are saved. If you connect a relay, one straight-on frame is posted to it and passed to the face provider, which estimates an age and returns a verdict; the relay discards the frame.

A passing check unlocks cash-out and a promotional boost on rewards. Failing it, or declining it, leaves codes, cashback cards and price history working — only cash-out waits. Clear the verdict in Settings to return to an unverified state; that locks cash-out again and does not delete anything a relay you configured holds.

Contact, and what to put in a message

Write to lumasupport@tidio.xyz. That address is the official contact point for a bug, a store that stopped working, a question about a payout, a data request, a complaint and a security report. It is the same address named in section 30 of the Terms, and it reaches the people who maintain the Extension.

A message that can be acted on usually says:

  • which store or page address you were on, and what you were trying to do;
  • which browser and version, and which version of the Extension (it is on this page, and in the browser's extensions page);
  • what the panel showed — the wording of the message, or a screenshot of the panel itself, with anything personal covered;
  • whether the same steps still reproduce it after a reload and a forced update.

Never send a password, a full card number, a security code, a recovery key, an export file or a coupon code you have not used. We do not need them, and a message containing them is a risk to you rather than help to us.

What we can and cannot do

We can read a defect report, reproduce it where a page is public, fix it in a release and tell you which version carries the fix. We can explain what the panel did and why, and point you at the switch or setting involved.

We cannot recover an account lost with a browser profile, open a sealed backup without its recovery key, read data that never left your device, reverse a store's decision about a code, an order, a refund or a chargeback, or make a payout provider approve a payout it declined. Those limits are the same ones the Terms describe; a message does not change them, and we would rather say so here than disappoint you later.

A security report is welcome and should be sent before anything is made public. We will not pursue a good-faith report made in that way, and we will say what we found and what changed.
