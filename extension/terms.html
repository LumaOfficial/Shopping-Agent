4. What the Extension does

The Extension adds a panel to your browser. What the panel can do for you is:

  • Find coupon codes. It reads the page you are shopping on for codes the store itself publishes, and can look up further codes from a source you configure.
  • Try codes for you. When you ask it to, it types a code into the store's own promo or coupon field and reports what the store page said happened. It never submits an order, never completes a checkout, and never pays for anything.
  • Show cashback offers. It shows rates published by cashback programmes and, when you tap Activate, opens the tracked link that hands your visit to that programme.
  • Show how a price has moved. It records prices on product pages you visit on this device, and can add a 90-day low from a product API if you configure one.
  • Show sponsored offers. It can show offers booked by advertisers, and says so on the card.
  • Block ad slots and show what they were worth. When the ad-blocking switch is on, it stops requests to advertising and tracker domains and hides the empty slots left behind in the shops it covers, then credits an estimated value to your wallet.
  • Keep a wallet. Savings, cashback and ad value are recorded against the account you create on this device, and can be paid out as a virtual card through a payout provider.

Each of these is switched on or off in the Extension's settings. Turning one off stops that one — the ad blocker stops blocking when switched off, and the other switches only hide their card.

That list is the whole of what the Extension does. It does not place an order, does not complete a checkout, does not pay for anything and does not act as your agent with a store. It does not compare every price on the internet, does not audit a store's pricing and does not tell you whether a purchase is a good idea. What it shows is what the pages and services it can reach published, at the moment it looked.

The catalog of shops the Extension covers ships with the package and is re-read hourly where a published list is configured. A shop can be added or removed, and a shop covered today may not be covered after an update. The panel tells you when a page it cannot work on is opened, and nothing is read from a page that is not covered.

A feature that depends on a provider you have not configured does nothing rather than guessing. Where a card cannot be filled in, an offer cannot be looked up or a price cannot be checked, the panel says which piece is missing, and the rest of the Extension keeps working.

5. Access to the sites you visit

What the Extension asks for.   • finding the card fields on a checkout page by their attributes only, so it can tell you your browser's own autofill is available; and

Those are the only purposes. It does not read or send your password fields, your card numbers, expiry dates or security codes, your messages, or the content of pages that are not shops it covers. Page scripts run only on the merchant domains the Extension ships with and the shops you add, plus the small ad-slot script described above. Nothing it reads is sent anywhere unless you have configured a partner address yourself, and then only the request that feature needs.

Advertising requests. Separately from page access, the ad-blocking switch in section 6 stops requests to advertising and tracker domains in the browser itself, which is a network rule and not a reading of the pages you visit.

The catalog, and what a page is. A page is treated as a covered shop only when its address matches an entry in the catalog or a shop you added.
