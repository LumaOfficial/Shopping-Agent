4. What the Extension does

The Extension adds a panel to your browser. What the panel can do for you is:

  • Find coupon codes. It reads the page you are shopping on for codes the store itself publishes, and can look up further codes from a source you configure.
  • Try codes for you. When you ask it to, it types a code into the store's own promo or coupon field and reports what the store page said happened. It never submits an order, never completes a checkout, and never pays for anything.
  • Show cashback offers. It shows rates published by cashback programmes and, when you tap Activate, opens the tracked link that hands your visit to that programme.
  • Show how a price has moved. It records prices on product pages you visit on this device, and can add a 90-day low from a product API if you configure one.
  • Show sponsored offers. It can show offers booked by advertisers, and says so on the card.
  • Block ad slots and show what they were worth. When the ad-blocking switch is on, it stops requests to advertising and tracker domains and hides the empty slots left behind in the shops it covers, then credits an estimated value to your wallet.
  • Keep a wallet. Savings, cashback and ad value are recorded against the account you create on this device, and can be paid out as a virtual card through a payout provider.

Each of these is switched on or off in the Extension's settings. Turning one off stops that one — the ad blocker stops blocking when switched off, and the other switches only hide their card.

5. Access to the sites you visit

What the Extension asks for. The Extension runs on the shops in the catalog it ships with, and on any shop you add yourself — adding one asks your browser for that single site's permission at the moment you tap the button. It holds no standing access to every website, and on a page that is not a covered shop it reads nothing from that page. Where that access is granted, it is what makes these specific features work:

  • recognising that the page you opened is a shop it covers;
  • reading coupon codes, coupon controls and product prices that the page publishes;
  • typing a code you chose into the store's own promo field, and reading back the store's own confirmation so it can tell you whether the code worked;
  • finding the card fields on a checkout page by their attributes only, so it can tell you your browser's own autofill is available; and
  • hiding the ad slots advertising left empty on pages where the ad-blocking switch is on, and counting them.

Those are the only purposes. This permission is not requested on the basis that a browser store requires it, and we do not claim that any store mandates it; it is requested because these features cannot work without it. If you would rather not grant it, do not install or do not agree, and the Extension will not run for you.

What it does not do with that access. It does not read or send your password fields, your card numbers, expiry dates or security codes, your messages, or the content of pages that are not shops it covers. Page scripts run only on the merchant domains the Extension ships with and the shops you add, plus the small ad-slot script described above. Nothing it reads is sent anywhere unless you have configured a partner address yourself, and then only the request that feature needs.

Advertising requests. Separately from page access, the ad-blocking switch in section 6 stops requests to advertising and tracker domains in the browser itself, which is a network rule and not a reading of the pages you visit.

6. How the Extension is paid for

The Extension is free to use. It is paid for by the commercial relationships that stand behind the offers it shows, and we tell you about each of them here rather than burying it:

  • Affiliate cashback. When you activate a cashback offer, the visit is handed to a cashback programme or merchant with a tracked link. If that visit results in a qualifying purchase, the programme pays commission, and a share of it is recorded to your wallet.
  • Sponsored offers. Advertisers can book offers that appear in the panel. Those cards are labelled as sponsored. Payment for a placement can affect whether a sponsored offer is shown and where it sits among other offers, but it never changes whether a code works or what a code saves you.
  • Ad value. With the ad-blocking switch on, the Extension stops advertising requests and credits an estimated share of the value of the impressions it prevented to your wallet. The figure is an estimate from a published rate, it is capped per page, per tab and per day, and it is a promotional credit from us — not money collected from an advertiser on your behalf.

Wallet balances are a record of credits we have awarded. They are not deposits, they earn no interest, they are not insured, they cannot be transferred to another person, and they may only be redeemed through the payout options the Extension offers, subject to the minimum and to the payout provider's own availability, identity checks and terms.

11. Age, the face-based check and the boost

By default the frames are measured on your device and are not uploaded to us or to anyone else; only the measurements stay, so the Extension can tell whether a check has been completed.
