What this policy covers, and what it does not

What stays on your device

Everything below is written to the Extension's own storage in the browser profile it is installed in. There is no Luma Pay server that holds an account, and no backup of one unless you seal a copy yourself and choose where it goes (see Encrypted backups you control below).

  • Your acceptance of the Terms. The version you agreed to (currently 1.4) and the time you agreed. A record without a real version and timestamp is not treated as agreement.
  • Your account. A profile on this device, not an online account. It holds the email address you signed up with, a phone number if you chose to give one, your date of birth for the 18+ gate, when the account was created, and — instead of your password — a salted PBKDF2-SHA-256 hash with the iteration count used to make it. Your password itself is never stored and never leaves the device.
  • Your session. Signing in records a session so the panel does not ask every time. It expires after a week without use and a month after it was opened, whichever comes first, so a shared machine does not stay signed in indefinitely. Signing out removes it.
  • Your settings. Which features are switched on, the shops you added yourself, and the partner addresses and credentials you entered for optional feeds — offer feed, cashback link template, payout provider, sponsored offers, price history and the age-check relay. Addresses are kept only if they are HTTPS or a loopback listener; a plain HTTP address on the open internet is dropped rather than sent a credential. Credentials are stored in this profile and sent only to the address you entered them for. The settings page also remembers which level you were viewing — simple, advanced or developer — as a page preference, and nothing else.
  • Codes. Codes read from a store's own page, and codes that store accepted, so the same code can be offered again next time you shop there.
  • Price points. Prices read on product pages you visited in covered shops, kept so the panel can show how a price has moved. Older entries are pruned as new ones are recorded.
  • Your wallet. Cashback, savings and ad value credited to your account, and the payout requests you started. These figures are a record of credits awarded, not a deposit account.
  • Age-check verdict. Whether a check was completed, the provider's name, its reference and the time. Never a frame and never a face template.
  • Sign-in guards. A counter of failed sign-in attempts and the time a lock applies, so repeated guesses are slowed down.

That list is the whole record. The Extension keeps no browsing history as a log, no keystrokes, no screenshots, no clipboard contents, no contact list and no location. There is no field for a card number, an expiry date or a security code, which is why those can never be stored by accident. Nothing on this list is shared between browser profiles, between devices or between people using the same device under different accounts, except where an export or a sealed backup you created carries it.

Where the Extension stores a credential you entered, it stores it in this profile and sends it only to the address you entered it for. A credential is never placed in an export, never sealed into a backup and never included in a request to a different address. Where a partner address is not HTTPS and is not a loopback listener, it is dropped rather than sent a secret.

Encrypted backups you control

A browser profile can be wiped, and with it everything listed above. Backup and recovery in the settings seals the whole profile — account, wallet, saved and verified codes, price points, settings and progress — into one code and locks it before it goes anywhere:

  • Locked on this device. AES-256-GCM, with the key derived from a 160-bit recovery key by 600,000 rounds of PBKDF2-SHA-256 over a fresh salt. No key is sent, stored on a server, or included in the code.
  • A destination you choose, or none. Save an encrypted copy writes a file you decide where to keep. If you also enter a sync address, the Extension posts the sealed code to that address after an earning and once an hour — and the host cannot read what it receives.
  • Not a copy of your credentials. Provider tokens and payout credentials are left out, so a restored device asks for those again.
  • Not ours. We hold no key, no escrow and no way to recover a code. If the recovery key is lost, the copy cannot be opened by anyone.

Restoring replaces what the profile holds and signs the device out; nothing is written until a code opens. You can remove a sealed copy at any time by deleting the file or the record at the address you chose, and the Extension keeps no other copy.

What leaves your device

Data leaves only in the five situations section 13 of the Terms lists. Each requires an address you configured yourself, and each carries only what that one feature needs:

  • A partner request — for example a store name and a page address, sent to the offers feed you configured so it can look up offers for that page, or a plain request for a published savings list you pointed the Extension at (which carries no account data at all);
  • A payout request you started — the details the payout provider requires to issue a virtual card, sent to the payout address you configured;
  • An age-check request — if, and only if, you connected an age-check relay (below);
  • An offer or product lookup — a product identifier or page address sent to a price-history or sponsored-offer address you configured;
  • A sealed backup — ciphertext only, to the sync address you entered, and only if you created a recovery key and filled that address in.

There is no Luma Pay analytics endpoint, no crash reporting, no advertising SDK and no telemetry, so nothing is sent to us merely because you used the Extension. The package restricts where data can go — scripts only from its own files, and connections only over HTTPS or to a loopback listener on this machine — so an address you have not configured is not a destination at all.

A request is built for the feature that sends it and carries nothing else: no account identifier, no wallet balance, no list of codes and no record of other pages. Where a provider's answer is stored, it is stored on this device as the offer, rate or price it describes. What a provider does with a request once it receives it is governed by that provider's own notice, which we do not control and cannot amend; if that matters to you, configure only providers whose notice you have read.

You can stop every outbound request by leaving the partner addresses empty and not creating a sync address. With no address configured, the Extension works from the catalog and the pages it can read, and sends nothing at all.

What is read on a web page, and what is never read

The Extension runs on the shops in the catalog it ships with, and on any shop you add yourself, which asks your browser for that one site's permission at the moment you tap the button. It holds no standing access to every website, and on a page that is not a covered shop it reads nothing from that page.

On a covered shop it reads only:

  • whether the page is a shop it covers;
  • coupon codes, coupon controls and product prices that the page publishes;
  • the store's own promo field, so it can type a code you chose and read back what the store said;
  • the attributes of card fields on a checkout page, so it can tell you your browser's autofill is available;
  • the empty ad slots left behind on pages where the ad-blocking switch is on, so it can hide them and count them.

It never reads or sends your password fields, card numbers, expiry dates or security codes, your messages, or the content of pages that are not covered shops.

The optional face-based age check

The 18+ check is optional and asks you separately before it starts. The camera opens only from the panel's own button, only after you tick an empty box, and the package asks for no camera permission. The stream is stopped when the scan finishes, when you cancel, when you sign out, and when the camera track ends.

With no relay configured — the default — nothing leaves the device. You turn your head to a few angles; the panel measures small copies of the camera picture on this device, compares the angles as a temporary face model, and drops them. No provider, no account, no key. Only the outcome, the words “On-device check” and the time are saved with your account.

If you connect an age-check relay, the consent facts on screen say so before the camera opens, and name the relay. In that case one straight-on frame is posted over HTTPS to the relay, which passes it to the face provider. The provider estimates an age from that frame, and the relay discards it — it writes nothing to disk and logs no image. The angles you turned through are still compared on this device and dropped. Only the outcome, the provider's name, its reference and the time are saved with your account.

An age estimate is a statistical guess, not proof of age and not an identity check. Declining the check leaves the rest of the Extension working; only the cash-out and its promotional boost stay locked.

Advertising and tracker requests

With the ad-blocking switch on, the Extension stops requests to advertising and tracker domains in the browser itself. That is a network rule applied to request addresses — not a reading of the pages you visit — so it records no browsing history for you or for us. Blocking begins only after you have agreed to the Terms, and switching ad blocking off stops the blocking, the ad-slot hiding and counting, and the ad-value credit.

Cookies, analytics and tracking

The Extension sets no cookies and contains no analytics, telemetry, fingerprinting or advertising SDK of its own, and it does not use advertising identifiers. Sponsored offers are labelled as sponsored in the panel; payment for a placement can affect whether such an offer is shown and where it sits, but it does not change whether a code works or what it saves you.

Selling, sharing and disclosure

We do not sell your data. Because the Extension holds your data on your device and has no account server, there is no store of it for us to sell, share or disclose. The only sharing that happens is the requests you configure, listed under What leaves your device, and any disclosure required by law in a case where we actually hold something — which, for the data described here, we do not.

Retention and deletion

Data stays in this browser profile until it is deleted there:

  • Session — expires a week after last use and a month after it was opened; signing out removes it immediately.
  • Price points — older entries are pruned as new ones are recorded.
  • Everything else — remains until you delete it.

Deleting it is the deletion. There is no server copy to remove. Clear saved codes and history in the settings deletes the signed-in account's shopping record — saved and verified codes, price points, savings and wallet — and leaves another account on the same browser untouched. Export a copy writes a file containing that same shopping record plus your settings, with every stored credential left out and kept on the device it was entered on. Sign out of this device ends the session. Removing the Extension from your browser, or clearing the browser profile's data, deletes the account, the wallet and everything else with it — and we cannot restore any of it.

A sealed backup is a copy of the profile that you placed somewhere. It is ciphertext, and it is deleted by deleting the file or the record at the address you chose — the Extension keeps no second copy and we hold no copy at all. A record at a sync address is deleted when you delete it there; a host that keeps its own logs or backups of what it received is outside our control, and its notice governs that.

Where a store or a provider retains something it received from a request, that retention is its own and is described in its own notice. Nothing in this policy shortens or extends it, and a message to us cannot delete it.

Security

  • Your password is stored only as a salted PBKDF2-SHA-256 hash (210,000 iterations), never in a form that can be read back, and it never leaves the device.
  • Repeated failed sign-ins are slowed by a growing lock-out.
  • Sessions expire on their own, so a copied session stops working.
  • The package runs only its own scripts; no remote code is loaded, and connections are limited to HTTPS or a loopback listener.
  • Partner addresses must be HTTPS or loopback before a credential is sent to them.
  • Page access is limited to the catalog's shops and the sites you add one at a time.

No system is perfect. Anyone with access to this browser profile and an open session can see the account's balances and act in it — if you share a machine, sign out when you are done, or clear the Extension's data in its settings. A sealed backup is only as private as its recovery key: keep the key somewhere other than beside the file, because a copy of both together is a copy of the profile.

Storage on the device is not encrypted at rest by the Extension beyond what your operating system does for the browser profile, so a device that is unlocked and unattended is a device whose profile can be read. Disk encryption, a screen lock and a separate browser profile for shopping are the controls that address that, and they are yours to set.

We do not claim that the Extension has been audited by a third party, that it is free of defects, or that a defect will be corrected within a particular time. What we do is fix what is reported and say in the release notes when a security-relevant change is made.

Legal bases, and the limits we set ourselves

The Extension does its work in your browser, on your instruction, and almost everything it handles never reaches us. Where a law calls for a legal basis, the handling rests on one or more of these, as the case requires:

  • Your agreement — the Terms you accepted, the separate agreement to the optional face-based check, and the consent your browser records when you add a shop or grant a site permission;
  • Performance of what you asked for — recognising a shop, reading codes, filling a promo field you chose, recording a price, issuing a payout you requested;
  • Legitimate interests — keeping the Extension working, preventing abuse of the wallet and of the rate limits, keeping a record of what a store's page said so you can see it, and answering a message you sent us, balanced against your rights and the fact that the data stays with you;
  • A legal obligation — where a payout provider, a tax rule, a sanctions rule or a consumer-protection rule requires a record to be kept or a check to be made.

We set limits on ourselves that the law does not always require: no profile of your browsing across shops, no advertising identifiers, no sale of data, no analytics endpoint, and no feature whose function depends on sending your page content to us. Those limits are part of how the Extension is built, and this policy describes them rather than promising them as a favour.

Automated processing

Three decisions in the Extension are made by a program rather than by a person: whether a page is a covered shop, whether a code a store's page accepted counts as verified, and whether an ad-value credit falls inside the per-page, per-tab and per-day caps. Each is mechanical, each is visible in the panel, and each can be overridden by the switches in the settings.

The optional age check produces an estimate of whether a person appears to be 18 or over, and that estimate can unlock a payout and a promotional boost. It is a statistical guess, not proof of age, not an identity check, and not a decision about your legal capacity. A failure of the check does not block the codes, cashback or price features, and it is not used to build a profile of you. You can ask for the check to be cleared, which returns the account to an unverified state.

No decision that has a legal effect on you beyond unlocking a payout is made about you by automated means, and no advertising profile is built from the pages you visit.

Browser signals, Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control

The Extension sends no tracking request that a Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signal would need to suppress, so it does not need to read those signals to honour them: there is nothing to opt out of. Where your browser offers other controls — third-party cookie blocking, a site permission for a shop, the Extension's own switches — those controls act directly on the Extension and are respected as they are set.

United States state privacy rights

Several state laws give residents rights over personal information: to know what is collected, to access it, to correct it, to delete it, to obtain a copy, to opt out of its sale or sharing, and not to be discriminated against for exercising those rights. The Extension is built so that most of that resolves on your own device:

A request that the controls do not cover can be sent to lumasupport@tidio.xyz; where we hold nothing to act on, the answer will say so and name the control that does apply. You may also contact your state attorney general, and nothing here limits a right your state's law gives you.

If something goes wrong

Because we hold no account server and no copy of your profile, a security problem at our end cannot expose data we never received. The exposure that matters is on your own device: a shared browser profile, a copied session, a browser extension with wider permissions than this one, or a sealed backup whose recovery key was stored next to the file.

If you think a defect in the Extension has exposed something, write to lumasupport@tidio.xyz before making it public. If the device is in someone else's hands, sign out, change the password of anything the browser stored, remove the Extension, and delete any sealed copy you kept.
